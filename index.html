About Say Hello Bristol

Hi, I'm Jenny Dance, a British English pronunciation coach based right here in Bristol, on Gloucester Road — pronounced /ˌɡlɒs.tə ˈrəʊd/ (Make ROAD the strongest part of this phrase: glos-tə ROAD).

For the last 20+ years I've coached international professionals on their spoken English, and I regularly find one thing: people with excellent English still hesitate over pronunciation. Bristol's place names are a good example of general English words (important everyday words, if you live here) which can be difficult to pronounce. I sometimes have to check with my students: 'Did you say Clifton or Cleavdon?' That relaxed /ɪ/ vowel in Clifton is not always easy to say right.

If you want to know if 'Leigh' rhymes with 'high' or 'see'; or how to say 'Cabot Circus' correctly, you've come to the right place! And even native speakers will struggle with the Bristol pub, the Llandoger Trow...

I built Say Hello Bristol — a map where you can hear how to actually say the street names, landmarks and neighbourhoods — to help visitors and international residents get around the city with confidence.

For each entry, you can see the phonetic spelling, get a quick tip on how to pronounce it, hear the model audio, and record yourself to compare.

Reading the phonetic spelling

Each card uses the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA) — symbols that represent sounds, not letters, so you always know exactly how a word is said. Two small marks help with rhythm too:

A mark before a syllable (ˈ) shows the stressed syllable — say it a little louder and stronger than the rest. A lighter mark (ˌ) shows a secondary, weaker stress. A dot (.) simply marks where one syllable ends and the next begins.

For example, in /ˌɡlɒs.tə ˈrəʊd/, the dot splits Gloucester into two syllables, the light mark shows a little stress there, and the strong mark shows that "road" carries the most emphasis.
